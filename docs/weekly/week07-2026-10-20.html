What MATSim does, and where each direction acts

The problem: random proposals, each evaluated by a full simulation

Concept

MATSim is a population of agents performing a noisy search for a stable state of a congestion game.

Population
389,301 agents. Each has a daily plan (activities, departure times, modes, routes) and keeps up to 5 plans with a score each.
Congestion game
An agent's score depends on everyone's plans: all plans share road and transit capacity in one traffic simulation.
Search
Each iteration about 30% of agents copy one of their plans and change its time, route or mode at random; the others choose among their stored plans by a logit on the scores.
Noise
A new plan is scored from a single execution, in traffic that includes other agents' trial plans; the new score replaces the old one, with no averaging.
Stable state
With innovation switched off, the choice process converges in distribution: values still fluctuate, their statistics stop changing (MATSim book, Ch. 48). More than one such state can exist. Operationally, we call a run stable when the policy effects stop changing (RQ3).

Next steps: three directions

1. Change the search, need fewer iterations

First, analyse the finished runs. Compare the plans each agent finally keeps with the plans tried and discarded on the way: which agents, which change (time, route, mode), at which iteration, from which parent. The data exist: replanning logs of the original and elite-parent runs, plan snapshots every 10 iterations, per-person records every iteration.

Then map the patterns to methods from other fields, to cut redundant trials and oscillation so that each agent reaches the plan it will finally need sooner:

  • propose by estimated utility or a predictor: POLARIS, MATSim's informed mode choice (4–5× fewer iterations to a similar score), surrogate screening in evolutionary computation;
  • search only where the solution is unstable: L2Seg, selective replanning;
  • react to averaged travel times to damp oscillation: MSA, fictitious play.
Risk
Existing mode-choice estimators ignore road tolls; published gains are measured by score over hundreds of iterations, not by policy effects in 100.

Even a free mobsim gives at most ≈ 4.5× per iteration; replanning then dominates.
Cheapest step
Allocate 4–6 cores per run instead of 16: estimated 2–3× fewer core-hours (not measured), but the original procedure gains equally, so it belongs to the baseline.

3. Predict the outcome with a model

Natterer et al. 2025 train a graph neural network on about 10,000 MATSim runs to predict policy-induced link volume changes: R² 0.91 overall, but 0.77 on roads the policy does not touch.

Problem
It replaces the final evaluation, while we need the settled state of a whole population; R² 0.77 on roads without capacity change; training required ≈ 10,000 MATSim runs.
Possible use
Screen which policies are worth simulating, or give direction 1 a prior for its proposals.
